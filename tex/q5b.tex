  \begin{abstract}
    The companion paper Q5a shows that, with the pipeline's initial vector, the canonical filtration of the admissible
    fibre on the Heisenberg carrier of $\HeisZ$ is a growing toric Fourier window, that the published admissibility
    forms converge to the zero form on it, and that no common scalar normalisation produces a non-trivial toric
    differential operator.
    The spatial input of the present paper is therefore not established; we formalise it as an explicit hypothesis
    [H-L] (existence of a spatial second-order limit operator $\LPi = -A\partial_x^2$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$) and state
    every result that consumes it conditionally on [H-L].
    We present three results.
    First, on balls of radius $n \ll \sqrt q$, where the BFS balls of $\Gq = \HeisZ$ coincide with those of the
    integer Heisenberg group, the rescaled BFS shells converge to the sphere foliation of a sub-Finsler
    Carnot--Car\-a\-th\'{e}o\-do\-ry metric on $\HeisR$; the homogeneous dimension $\Dhom = 4$
    (Bass--Guivarc'h) gives the limiting geometry the volume growth and spectral dimension of a four-dimensional
    space.
    Second, under [H-L] and the lifting hypothesis [H-lift], which identifies $\LPi$ with $A$ times the kinetic sector
    of the nonnegative operator $-\DeltaH$ under the Schr\"{o}dinger representation, the principal symbol of the
    effective operator on
    $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$ gives a leading-order co-metric $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, 0)$ in the
    left-invariant frame.
    Hypothesis [H-lift] is open: Q9 gives sufficient conditions for a kinetic Mosco limit and does not discharge it.
    The central slot is empty and cannot be filled by lower-order terms, since the sub-Laplacian has no first-order
    part and a rank-two principal symbol; a full-rank extension requires a new operator (open problem Q5b-O2).
    No value of $A_\tau$, $A_H$ or of a central coefficient $A_z$ is established: Q10 derives no value of $A_H$, Q8
    shows that invariance leaves the common value of an isotropic form free, and the temporal value asserted in Q11
    rests on the same Casimir normalisation.
    Third, conditionally on the same two hypotheses and on the hyperbolicity hypothesis [H-hyp] of the companion
    signature analysis, applied to the sector of the effective operator that does not depend on the central
    variable, the signature of the non-degenerate block is $(-,+,+)$ with $\tau$ time-like; an extension
    $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, A_z)$ with $A_z > 0$ in the left-invariant frame would be Lorentzian.
    Each result carries an explicit status: structural, or conditional on named hypotheses.
    Interpretive outlook: the four-dimensionality of the emergent space is carried by the homogeneous dimension of
    the Heisenberg carrier, while its metric completion, the length of the central direction and the values of the
    coefficients remain open; Q5 is open.
  \end{abstract}

  \subsection{Context and the status of the Q5a input}

    Three data must be distinguished, and this paper supplies rather than derives each of them.
    The finite group $\HeisZ$ for a prime $q$ is stipulated in the notation conventions below and
    used throughout.
    A Heisenberg carrier for it --- the Heisenberg--Schr\"odinger representation $\pi_c$ on
    $\mathcal{H}_c = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ at a fixed non-trivial central character --- is a
    supplied input, and identifying the admissible fibre $F_n$ with $\mathcal{H}_c$ is a modelling
    dictionary rather than a theorem.
    The associated Weil action, the representation of the symplectic automorphism group of the same
    carrier, is a \emph{further} datum again; it is not needed for any result below.
    None of this is selected by the admissibility axioms: they force irreducibility and
    non-commutation, but neither make the commutator central nor single out a finite Heisenberg
    group, an explicit $\mathfrak{S}_3$ countermodel satisfying the extracted carrier
    contract~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
    Since the group is stipulated and the geometry below is that of $\HeisR$, no result of this paper
    depends on how the carrier is obtained.
    The Cosmochrony white paper~\cite{Cosmochrony} identifies three qualitative convergences
    expected to hold in the large-$q$ limit:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(i)] The group $\Gq = \HeisZ$ and its representation space $\Cq \simeq L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
      approach $\HeisR$ and $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.
      \item[(ii)] The BFS shell structure of $\Gq$ approaches a continuous foliation, with
      polynomial ball growth $|\Bn| \sim c\,n^4$ inducing a four-dimensional effective geometry.
      \item[(iii)] The discrete admissibility filter $\Pi_q$ approaches a second-order differential
      constraint, recovering the Born--Infeld Lagrangian in the continuum.
    \end{enumerate}
    The companion paper Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a} addresses convergences (i) and (iii).
    It proves that, with the pipeline's initial vector, the canonical filtration of the
    admissible fibre is exactly a growing toric Fourier window; that the published
    admissibility form converges to the zero form on this filtration; and that, if the
    saturation depth diverges and the measured weights have positive finite limits, no common
    scalar normalisation of the form produces a non-trivial toric differential operator.
    In particular, no operator $-A\partial_x^2$ on a spatial $L^2$ space is currently derived
    anywhere in the corpus, and question Q5 is open.

    The spatial input of this paper is therefore an explicit hypothesis:
    \begin{hypothesis}[{[H-L]}: spatial limit input; not established]
      \label{hyp:HL}
      There exists a normalisation of the admissibility structure under which the
      admissibility forms converge to a second-order operator $\LPi = -A\partial_x^2$ on
      $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ with $A > 0$.
    \end{hypothesis}
    Hypothesis~[H-L] is not established; Q5a shows that it fails for the published
    normalisation and for every common scalar rescaling of it on toric targets.
    No source of [H-L] is identified in the corpus.
    The dual-window limit of Q5a is a statement in the dual space and produces no spatial continuum, and the
    balanced-scale route of Q5a would require the critical coverage $x_1(q)$ to stay bounded away from zero, whereas
    the critical-coverage note proves $x_1(q) \asymp q^{-2}$, deterministically for $q \ge 311$~\cite{Beau2026ccnote}.
    All results below that consume $\LPi$ are stated conditionally on [H-L]; the geometric
    convergence results (Theorems~\ref{thm:bass}--\ref{thm:carnot}) are independent of it.
    The present paper delivers the geometric reading in this conditional form.
    Convergence~(ii) and the associated structural results are the primary content.
    The results combine classical geometric convergence theorems, structural consequences
    of the Cosmochrony framework, and conditional identifications stated explicitly as
    hypotheses; each result carries an unambiguous status label throughout.

  \subsection{Scope and main results}

    The paper is organised around three contributions:

    \medskip
    \noindent\textbf{Carnot convergence (Theorem~\ref{thm:carnot}; structural).}
    We show that the BFS stratification $\{\Sn\}$ of $\Gq$ converges, on balls of radius $n \ll \sqrt q$, to the
    sphere foliation of the sub-Finsler Carnot--Car\-a\-th\'{e}o\-do\-ry metric of $\HeisR$ determined by the
    generating set.
    The homogeneous dimension $\Dhom = 4$ (Bass--Guivarc'h~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973})
    gives the limiting geometry the spectral dimension of a four-dimensional space.
    This result is structural: it follows from the theory of asymptotic cones of nilpotent
    groups (Pansu~\cite{Pansu1983}) and does not require any new hypothesis.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textbf{Metric extraction (Theorem~\ref{thm:metric}; conditional on [H-L] and [H-lift]).}
    Under [H-L] and Hypothesis~[H-lift] (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:lift}, open),
    $\LPi = -A\partial_x^2$ is
    $A$ times the image, under the Schr\"{o}dinger representation, of the kinetic sector of the
    nonnegative operator $-\DeltaH$, with $\DeltaH = \tilde X^2 + \tilde Y^2$ the sub-Laplacian on $\HeisR$,
    restricted to the admissible sector.
    The full effective operator on the four-dimensional space $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$
    has a principal symbol $A^{\mu\nu} k_\mu k_\nu$ from which the effective co-metric tensor
    $g^{\mu\nu} \propto A^{\mu\nu}$ is read off; it has rank three, with an empty central slot.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textbf{Signature (Theorem~\ref{thm:lorentz}; conditional on [H-L], [H-lift]
  and on the hyperbolicity hypothesis of~\cite{Beau2026c}).}
    The hyperbolicity hypothesis [H-hyp] of~\cite{Beau2026c}, applied to the sector of $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ that does
    not depend on the central variable, gives the signature $(-,+,+)$ on the non-degenerate block, with $\tau$
    time-like; an extension $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, A_z)$ with $A_z > 0$ would be Lorentzian.

    Status labels follow the convention of~\cite{Beau2026q5a}: ``proved'' denotes a
    complete proof in the cited companion; ``structural'' denotes a consequence of classical

  \section{\texorpdfstring{Sub-Riemannian Structure of $\HeisR$}{Sub-Riemannian Structure of Heis3(R)}}
  \label{sec:heis}

  \subsection{The Heisenberg group as a Carnot group}

    The real Heisenberg group $\HeisR$ is the Lie group $\mathbb{R}^3$ equipped with the
    group law
    \begin{equation}
      (x,y,z) \cdot (x',y',z') = \bigl(x+x',\; y+y',\; z + z' + \tfrac{1}{2}(x'y - xy')\bigr).
      \label{eq:heis-law}
    \end{equation}
    For this law, $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\, g\cdot(t,0,0)$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\, g\cdot(0,t,0)$ give the
    fields below, so its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}$ is spanned by the left-invariant vector fields
    \begin{equation}
      \tilde X = \partial_x + \tfrac{y}{2}\,\partial_z, \qquad
      \tilde Y = \partial_y - \tfrac{x}{2}\,\partial_z, \qquad
      \tilde Z = \partial_z,
      \label{eq:generators}
    \end{equation}
    satisfying $[\tilde X, \tilde Y] = -\tilde Z$ and $[\tilde X, \tilde Z] =
  [\tilde Y, \tilde Z] = 0$.
    The Lie algebra admits a two-step stratification:
    \begin{equation}
      \mathfrak{h} = \mathfrak{g}_1 \oplus \mathfrak{g}_2, \qquad
      \mathfrak{g}_1 = \mathrm{span}\{\tilde X, \tilde Y\}, \qquad \mathfrak{g}_2 =
      \mathrm{span}\{\tilde Z\}.
    \end{equation}
    The horizontal distribution $\mathcal{H} \subset T\HeisR$ is the left-invariant rank-2
    distribution generated by $\mathfrak{g}_1$; it satisfies the H\"{o}rmander bracket
    condition $\mathfrak{g}_1 + [\mathfrak{g}_1, \mathfrak{g}_1] = \mathfrak{h}$,
    ensuring that any two points are connected by a horizontal curve (Chow--Rashevskii
    theorem).
    The central direction $\tilde Z = \partial_z$, generated by the commutator $[\tilde X,
  \tilde Y]$, is not horizontal; it spans the second layer $\mathfrak{g}_2$ and carries
    weight~$2$ under the anisotropic dilations~\eqref{eq:dilations}.
    This weight-$2$ status is what makes the central direction sub-dominant in the horizontal
    geometry while contributing a full unit to the homogeneous dimension.

  \subsection{Anisotropic dilations and homogeneous dimension}

    The group $\HeisR$ admits a one-parameter family of anisotropic dilations
    \begin{equation}
      \delta_r(x, y, z) = (r x,\; r y,\; r^2 z), \qquad r > 0,
      \label{eq:dilations}
    \end{equation}
    which are group homomorphisms and assign weights $1$ to $\tilde X$ and $\tilde Y$,
    and weight $2$ to $\tilde Z$.
    The \emph{homogeneous dimension} is defined as
    \begin{equation}
      \Dhom = \sum_i \mathrm{weight}_i \cdot \dim(\mathfrak{g}_i)
      = 1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 1 = 4.
      \label{eq:dhom}
    \end{equation}

    \begin{theorem}[Ball growth]
      \label{thm:bass}
      For the discrete Heisenberg group $\HeisZZ$ with the standard symmetric generating set
      $\{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$, the BFS ball satisfies $c_1 n^4 \le |B_n(\HeisZZ)| \le c_2 n^4$
      (Bass~\cite{Bass1972}, Guivarc'h~\cite{Guivarch1973}) and
      \begin{equation}
        |B_n(\HeisZZ)| \sim C \, n^4 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty
      \end{equation}
      (Pansu~\cite{Pansu1983}), for constants $0 < c_1 \le C \le c_2$.
      The central coordinate of an element of $B_n(\HeisZZ)$ is at most $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ in absolute value, and
      if $q > \max(2n, 2\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor)$ the reduction $\HeisZZ \to \Gq$ is injective on
      $B_n$~\cite{Beau2026ccnote}.
      It then maps $B_n(\HeisZZ)$ bijectively onto $\Bn(\Gq)$ and preserves word length from the identity, so the
      two balls have the same cardinality and the same growth when $n \ll \sqrt q$; distances between arbitrary points
      of the ball are preserved when the condition holds at radius $2n$.
    \end{theorem}

    The exponent $4 = \Dhom$ is the Bass--Guivarc'h homogeneous dimension of $\HeisR$.
    Because the dilations~\eqref{eq:dilations} scale the Haar measure of a ball of radius $r$ exactly by $r^4$,
    the Hausdorff dimension of $\HeisR$ for any homogeneous Carnot--Carath\'eodory metric is $4$.
    Consequently, $(\HeisR, \dCC)$ has Hausdorff dimension $4$, matching the spectral
    properties of a four-dimensional physical space (see Section~\ref{sec:carnot}).

  \subsection{Carnot--Car\'{a}th\'{e}odory metric}

    Fix a left-invariant sub-Riemannian metric $g_{\mathcal{H}}$ on $\mathcal{H}$ making
    $\{\tilde X, \tilde Y\}$ orthonormal.
    The Carnot--Car\-a\-th\'{e}o\-do\-ry distance between two points $p, q \in \HeisR$ is
    \begin{equation}
      \dCC(p,q) = \inf_\gamma \int_0^1 \|\dot\gamma(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,dt,
    \end{equation}
    where the infimum is over all horizontal curves $\gamma$ from $p$ to $q$
    (curves whose velocity lies in $\mathcal{H}$ at every point).
    This metric is finite by Chow--Rashevskii and is left-invariant under the group action.
    The balls $B_r^{\dCC}(e) = \{g \in \HeisR : \dCC(e,g) \leq r\}$ satisfy
    $\mathrm{Vol}(B_r^{\dCC}) \sim C\,r^4$ as $r \to \infty$, consistently with~\eqref{eq:dhom}.
    The same construction with the $\ell^1$ norm $|a| + |b|$ on horizontal velocities $a\tilde X + b\tilde Y$ in
    place of the Euclidean one gives a sub-Finsler Carnot--Carath\'eodory metric $d_\infty$.
    The two metrics are bi-Lipschitz equivalent, so they share the Hausdorff dimension $4$; $\dCC$ is the metric of
    the sub-Laplacian below, and $d_\infty$ is the limit of the word metric in Section~\ref{sec:carnot}.

  \subsection{The sub-Riemannian Laplacian}

    The \emph{Kohn--Laplacian} (sub-Laplacian) is the second-order differential operator
    \begin{equation}
      \DeltaH = \tilde X^2 + \tilde Y^2
      = \Bigl(\partial_x + \tfrac{y}{2}\partial_z\Bigr)^2 +
      \Bigl(\partial_y - \tfrac{x}{2}\partial_z\Bigr)^2.
      \label{eq:sublaplacian}
    \end{equation}
    It is left-invariant, homogeneous of degree $2$ under $\delta_r$ in the sense that
    $\DeltaH(f\circ\delta_r) = r^2\,(\DeltaH f)\circ\delta_r$, and hypoelliptic (H\"{o}rmander's theorem).
    It has no first-order part: the coefficient $y/2$ of $\tilde X$ depends on neither $x$ nor $z$, and the
    coefficient $x/2$ of $\tilde Y$ on neither $y$ nor $z$.
    Its principal symbol at $p = (x,y,z)$ and covector $\xi = (\xi_x, \xi_y, \xi_z)$ is
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_2(\DeltaH)(p,\xi)
      = \Bigl(\xi_x + \tfrac{y}{2}\xi_z\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(\xi_y - \tfrac{x}{2}\xi_z\Bigr)^2.
      \label{eq:symbol-sublaplacian}
    \end{equation}
    This symbol is positive semi-definite of rank two at every point, with kernel spanned by
    $(-\tfrac{y}{2}, \tfrac{x}{2}, 1)$, reflecting the sub-Riemannian structure; its $\xi_z^2$ coefficient
    $(x^2+y^2)/4$ is position-dependent and vanishes at the identity.
    The operator $-\DeltaH$ is essentially self-adjoint on $L^2(\HeisR)$ and its heat kernel
    satisfies
    \begin{equation}
      p_t(e,e) \sim C\,t^{-\Dhom/2} = C\,t^{-2} \quad \text{as } t \to 0^+,
    \end{equation}
    which is the same decay rate as the heat kernel on $\mathbb{R}^4$.
    This is the precise sense in which $(\HeisR, \DeltaH)$ has \emph{spectral dimension~4}.

  \section{BFS Stratification and Carnot Convergence}
  \label{sec:carnot}

  \subsection{Asymptotic cone of the Heisenberg group}

    The following theorem is the cornerstone of the BFS-to-Carnot identification.

    \begin{theorem}[Pansu~\cite{Pansu1983}]
      \label{thm:pansu}
      Let $\Gamma = \HeisZZ$ with word metric $d_w$ associated to the generating set
      $S = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$.
      For every $n \geq 1$, define the rescaled metric space
      $(\Gamma, n^{-1}d_w)$.
      As $n \to \infty$, this sequence of metric spaces converges in the Gromov--Hausdorff
      sense to $(\HeisR, d_\infty)$, the Carnot--Carath\'eodory metric whose horizontal unit ball is the convex hull of
      the projected generators, here the $\ell^1$ ball.
      More precisely, for any $R > 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $N_0$ such that
      for all $n \geq N_0$, the ball $\{g \in \Gamma : d_w(e,g) \leq nR\}$ equipped with
      the metric $n^{-1}d_w$ is $\varepsilon$-close to $B_R^{d_\infty}(e)$ in the
      Gromov--Hausdorff distance.
    \end{theorem}

  \subsection{Application to the finite Heisenberg group}

    The finite group $\Gq = \HeisZ$ differs from $\HeisZZ$ only through the identification
    $k \equiv k + q$ in each coordinate direction.
    The central coordinate reaches $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ on $B_n$, so the identification stays inactive only while
    $q > \max(2n, 2\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:bass}); in that range the reduction is injective on the
    BFS ball and preserves word length from the identity, and under the same condition at radius $2n$ the ball
    $\Bn(\Gq)$ is isometric to $\Bn(\HeisZZ)$.

    \begin{theorem}[Carnot convergence; structural]
      \label{thm:carnot}
      Fix $R > 0$.
      In the double limit $q \to \infty$, $n = \lfloor q^\alpha \rfloor$ with $0 < \alpha < 1/2$,
      the rescaled BFS metric space $(\Gq, n^{-1}d_w)$ restricted to balls of radius $R$
      converges in the Gromov--Hausdorff sense to the sub-Finsler Carnot--Car\-a\-th\'{e}o\-do\-ry
      ball $(B_R^{d_\infty}(e) \subset \HeisR, d_\infty)$.
      The homogeneous dimension $\Dhom = 4$ gives the limiting space the volume growth and
      spectral dimension of a four-dimensional metric space.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      Since $\alpha < 1/2$, for large $q$ the radius $2nR$ satisfies $q > \max(4nR, 2\lfloor (2nR)^2/4\rfloor)$, so
      the reduction is injective on the ball of radius $2nR$ and the ball of radius $nR$ in $\Gq$ is isometric to
      that of $\HeisZZ$, distances between its points being realised by words of length at most $2nR$
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:bass}).
      Theorem~\ref{thm:pansu} applied to $\Gamma = \HeisZZ$ then gives the Gromov--Hausdorff
      convergence at scale $n$.
      The Hausdorff dimension of $(\HeisR, d_\infty)$ and the spectral dimension of $(\HeisR, \DeltaH)$ equal
      $\Dhom = 4$ by~\eqref{eq:dhom}, the bi-Lipschitz equivalence with $\dCC$, and the heat-kernel asymptotics of
      Section~\ref{sec:heis}.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Effective four-dimensional foliation}

    The BFS stratification $\{\Sn\}_{n \geq 0}$ of $\Gq$ is a partition of $\Gq$ by
    ``shells'' at fixed word-metric distance from the identity.
    In the large-$q$ limit, Theorem~\ref{thm:carnot} identifies this stratification with
    the foliation of $(\HeisR, d_\infty)$ by its spheres
    $\partial B_r^{d_\infty}(e)$ at increasing radii.

    \begin{remark}[The four-dimensional reading]
      \label{rem:4d}
      In Cosmochrony, the observable spacetime is the product
      $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$, where $\tau$ denotes the projective ordering
      index (BFS depth)~\cite{Beau2026pto,Cosmochrony}.
      As a smooth manifold, $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR \cong \mathbb{R}^4$.
      The product structure is a modelling choice of this paper: it separates the projective ordering
      direction~$\tau$, for which a monotone proxy is supported numerically~\cite{Beau2026pto}, from the relational
      spatial structure encoded by $\HeisR$, the continuum form of the supplied Heisenberg carrier, which the axioms
      do not select~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
      Neither the ordering direction nor its coefficient is derived here.
      The BFS shells $\{\Sn\}$ foliate this product: for fixed $\tau = n$, each shell is a
      copy of the sphere $\partial B_n^{d_\infty}$ in the Heis$_3$ factor.
      The homogeneous dimension $\Dhom = 4$ of $\HeisR$ means that the spatial factor
      $\HeisR$ alone carries the volume growth and heat-kernel decay of a four-dimensional
      space.
      The full effective spacetime is therefore four-dimensional in the topological sense
      (as $\mathbb{R}^4$) and also carries a four-dimensional spectral structure in the
      spatial factor alone.
      This is the structural content of convergence~(ii) from the Cosmochrony white
      paper~\cite{Cosmochrony}.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Relation to the O-series data]
O9~\cite{Beau2026a13} reports, for exact BFS at the primes $q \in \{11, \ldots, 29\}$, a fitted ball-growth
exponent $\hat D$ increasing monotonically towards $4$.
The critical-coverage note~\cite{Beau2026ccnote} shows that the O-series observable saturates at a bounded depth,
at most $22$ for $q \ge 311$.
These data are consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:carnot} but, on windows of this size, do not test its asymptotic
statement.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Lifting Hypothesis and Sub-Riemannian Operator}
  \label{sec:lift}

  \subsection{The Schr\"{o}dinger representation and the 1D shadow}

    The sub-Laplacian $\DeltaH$ acts on functions $f(x,y,z)$ of three variables.
    Hypothesis~[H-L], however, posits a \emph{one-dimensional} operator
    $\LPi = -A\partial_x^2$ acting on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ (not derived by Q5a; see
    Section~\ref{sec:intro}).
    Bridging these two objects is the central open step of this paper: we identify the
    natural candidate for this bridge (the Schr\"{o}dinger representation), formulate a
    precise hypothesis [H-lift] in Section~\ref{ssec:hlift}, and give structural evidence.
    Hypothesis~[H-lift] is open: Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} gives sufficient conditions for a kinetic Mosco limit and states
    that its results do not discharge [H-lift].

    The connection between $\DeltaH$ and one-dimensional operators runs through the
    Schr\"{o}dinger representation.

    For each $\xi \neq 0$, the Schr\"{o}dinger representation
    $\pi_\xi : \HeisR \to \mathcal{U}(L^2(\mathbb{R}))$ is defined by
    \begin{equation}
      (\pi_\xi(x,y,z) f)(k) = e^{2\pi i \xi (yk + xy/2 - z)} f(k + x), \qquad f \in L^2(\mathbb{R}),
      \label{eq:schrodinger}
    \end{equation}
    a unitary representation for the group law~\eqref{eq:heis-law}.
    The infinitesimal generators act as
    \begin{equation}
      d\pi_\xi(\tilde X) = \partial_k, \qquad
      d\pi_\xi(\tilde Y) = 2\pi i \xi k, \qquad
      d\pi_\xi(\tilde Z) = -2\pi i \xi \, \mathrm{Id},
      \label{eq:generators-rep}
    \end{equation}
    consistently with $[\tilde X, \tilde Y] = -\tilde Z$.
    Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} orients the central generator oppositely; no statement about $\DeltaH$ depends on this
    orientation.
    Under this representation, the sub-Laplacian becomes
    \begin{equation}
      d\pi_\xi(\DeltaH) = d\pi_\xi(\tilde X)^2 + d\pi_\xi(\tilde Y)^2
      = \partial_k^2 - (2\pi\xi)^2 k^2.
      \label{eq:harmonic-osc}
    \end{equation}
    This is the quantum harmonic oscillator (up to sign and scaling).
    The two terms correspond to:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item $\partial_k^2$: the \emph{kinetic} sector, acting purely as $-(-\partial_k^2)$;
      \item $-(2\pi\xi)^2 k^2$: the \emph{potential} sector, growing with $k^2$.
    \end{itemize}
    The operator $\LPi = -A\partial_x^2$ posited by Hypothesis~[H-L] corresponds to (a multiple of)
    the kinetic sector, in the variable $k \equiv x$.
    It is not produced by Q5a, which proves a zero-form limit for its published construction, so $\LPi$
    enters here only through~[H-L]~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.

  \subsection{Admissibility filter and sector dominance}

    The represented operator $-d\pi_\xi(\DeltaH) = -\partial_k^2 + (2\pi\xi)^2 k^2$ is the harmonic oscillator:
    both of its terms are nonnegative, and the potential term $(2\pi\xi)^2 k^2$ is the modulation energy.
    Hypothesis~[H-L] posits a limit $A\int |\partial_x f|^2\,dx$, which contains only the kinetic term.
    For that limit to agree with the represented sub-Laplacian, the modulation energy has to vanish along the
    sequences realising the limit.
    Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} states sufficient conditions of exactly this kind: if the kinetic forms Mosco-converge [K]
    and every finite-energy limit vector admits one kinetic recovery sequence on which the modulation energy
    vanishes [R], then the sum Mosco-converges to the kinetic form (Q9 Theorem~1.2).
    The resulting form is the free kinetic form, not the full oscillator form, and it does not select a central
    character.
    Neither [K] nor [R] is derived from the admissibility data, and Q9 states that its results do not discharge
    [H-lift].
    A bound on the position moment of admissible vectors gives an elementwise kinetic lower bound only; its
    admissible class is a cone, not a linear form domain (Q9 Section~5).

  \subsection{The lifting hypothesis}
    \label{ssec:hlift}

    \begin{hypothesis}[{[H-lift]}]
      \label{hyp:lift}
      The operator $\LPi = -A\partial_x^2$ posited by Hypothesis~[H-L]
      (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:HL}) is $A$ times the image, under the Schr\"{o}dinger representation
      $\pi_1$ at unit central character $\xi = 1$, of the kinetic sector of the nonnegative operator $-\DeltaH$ on
      $\HeisR$, restricted to the admissible (low-energy) subspace of $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.
      Equivalently, $-\DeltaH$ is the pre-image (under $\pi_1$) of the oscillator $-\partial_k^2 + (2\pi)^2 k^2$,
      and $\LPi/A$ is its kinetic component $-\partial_k^2$ in the admissible sector.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{remark}[{Justification of [H-lift]}]
      \label{rem:hlift-justification}
      Hypothesis~[H-lift] is supported by three structural observations:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item The rescaled Heisenberg generators $\hat X_q / \sqrt{q}$, $\hat Y_q / \sqrt{q}$ are the natural
        discrete counterparts of the infinitesimal Schr\"odinger generators $d\pi_1(\tilde X) = \partial_k$ and
        $d\pi_1(\tilde Y) = 2\pi i k$, which enter $\DeltaH$ via~\eqref{eq:generators-rep}; no convergence of the
        former to the latter is established in the corpus, and this item is structural motivation only.
        \item The limit posited by [H-L] contains only the kinetic sector.
        For it to agree with the represented sub-Laplacian, the modulation energy must vanish along recovery
        sequences, which is premise [R] of Q9 (Section~\ref{sec:lift}.2).
        \item The operator identity $-d\pi_\xi(\DeltaH) = -\partial_k^2 + (2\pi\xi)^2 k^2$
        shows that the kinetic sector of $-\DeltaH$ is $-\partial_k^2$, and $\LPi = A\,(-\partial_x^2)$ has the same
        form under $k \equiv x$, the constant $A > 0$ absorbing the central character $\xi$ and the normalisation of
        the admissibility forms.
      \end{enumerate}
      The main open step is a direct proof that the Mosco limit of $\Eq$ coincides with the
      kinetic sector of $-d\pi_1(\DeltaH)$, up to the factor $A$, modulo the potential contribution, which is
      deferred to open problem Q5b-O1.
      This lifting should be understood as a representation-theoretic correspondence
      rather than a pointwise identification of operators: it asserts that
      $\LPi$ and $A$ times the kinetic sector of $-d\pi_1(\DeltaH)$ define the same nonnegative quadratic
      form on the admissible subspace of $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, not that they are equal
      as unbounded operators on all of $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.
      This correspondence is understood in a weak (spectral or representation-theoretic)
      sense rather than as a pointwise operator identity; in particular, the domains
      may differ outside the admissible subspace.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Scope of the harmonic analysis]
      \label{rem:harmonic-analysis}
      A full derivation of [H-lift] would require the Plancherel formula for $\HeisR$
      (decomposing $L^2(\HeisR)$ as a direct integral of Schr\"{o}dinger representations
      $\pi_\xi$, $\xi \neq 0$), together with a careful tracking of how the admissibility
      filter $\Pi_q$ acts on each fiber $\xi$.
      This analysis is not developed here.
      The present paper formulates [H-lift] as a precise structural hypothesis and provides
      the evidence described in Remark~\ref{rem:hlift-justification}; the complete harmonic
      analysis is deferred to future work.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Principal Symbol and Effective Metric Tensor}
  \label{sec:metric}

  \subsection{\texorpdfstring{The full effective operator on $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$}{The full effective
  operator on R x Heis3(R)}}

    Under [H-lift], the admissibility Dirichlet form in the continuum limit corresponds
    to the kinetic sector of $-\DeltaH$ acting on the spatial factor $\HeisR$.
    The complete effective operator on the four-dimensional space
    $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$ incorporates both the projective ordering direction
    $\tau$ and the Heisenberg spatial directions:
    \begin{equation}
      L_{\mathrm{eff}} = -A_\tau \partial_\tau^2 + A_H \DeltaH,
      \label{eq:leff}
    \end{equation}
    where the temporal coefficient $A_\tau > 0$ and the temporal term itself are supplied, the projective ordering
    being supported numerically by a monotone proxy~\cite{Beau2026pto}, and $A_H > 0$ is the spatial coefficient of
    [H-lift] (related to the constant $A$ in $\LPi = -A\partial_x^2$).
    The signs in~\eqref{eq:leff} are stipulated; Section~\ref{sec:lorentz} records under which hypothesis they are
    the only ones compatible with a finite characteristic cone ordered by $\tau$.

    \begin{remark}[Relation to the one-dimensional operator]
      \label{rem:shadow}
On the spatial slice $\{\tau = \text{const}\}$, the operator $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ reduces to
$A_H \DeltaH$.
In the Schr\"{o}dinger representation at $\xi = 1$, the kinetic sector of $A_H \DeltaH$
maps to $A_H \partial_k^2$, which is $-\LPi = A \partial_x^2$ upon relabeling
$k \to x$ and identifying $A_H = A$.
This is the reading of $\LPi$ as a ``one-dimensional shadow'' used in Q6b~\cite{Beau2026q6b}, which writes
$\mathcal{L}_\Pi$ for this one-dimensional operator and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for the four-dimensional one,
as the present paper does.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Principal symbol and metric extraction}

    Let $p = (\tau, x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$ and
    $k = (k_\tau, k_x, k_y, k_z)$ be the dual (momentum) covector.
    The principal symbol of $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at $p$ and $k$ is
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})(p, k) = -A_\tau k_\tau^2 + A_H \sigma_2(\DeltaH)(p, k_x, k_y, k_z),
      \label{eq:symbol-leff}
    \end{equation}
    where $\sigma_2(\DeltaH)$ is given by~\eqref{eq:symbol-sublaplacian}.
    In coordinates, this reads
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})(p,k)
      = -A_\tau k_\tau^2
      + A_H \Bigl[\Bigl(k_x + \tfrac{y}{2}k_z\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(k_y - \tfrac{x}{2}k_z\Bigr)^2\Bigr].
      \label{eq:symbol-expanded}
    \end{equation}
    Following the operator--metric correspondence of~\cite{Beau2026c} (see
  also~\cite{Beau2026a}), the effective metric tensor $g^{\mu\nu}(p)$ is read off
    from the principal symbol by
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}})(p,k) = g^{\mu\nu}(p)\,k_\mu k_\nu.
      \label{eq:op-metric}
    \end{equation}
    This defines $g^{\mu\nu}$ as a symmetric tensor on $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$.
    To spell out the identification: the sub-Laplacian $\DeltaH = \tilde X^2 + \tilde Y^2$
    is a sum of squares of horizontal vector fields, hence its principal symbol
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_2(\DeltaH)(p,\xi) = \sum_{i \in \{x,y\}}
      (\tilde v_i(p) \cdot \xi)^2, \qquad \tilde v_x = \tilde X,\; \tilde v_y = \tilde Y,
      \label{eq:symbol-as-sum}
    \end{equation}
    defines a positive semi-definite quadratic form on $T^*\HeisR$ whose matrix
    \[
      A^{jk}_H(p) = \sum_{i \in \{x,y\}} [\tilde v_i(p)]^j [\tilde v_i(p)]^k
    \]
    is the \emph{horizontal co-metric tensor} of the sub-Riemannian structure.
    The full symbol of $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ adds the temporal direction with a negative
    coefficient $-A_\tau$, consistently with the hyperbolic structure hypothesised
    in Section~\ref{sec:lorentz}:
    \begin{equation}
      g^{\mu\nu}(p) = \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau) \oplus A_H \cdot A^{ij}_H(p).
      \label{eq:full-cometric}
    \end{equation}
    This identification follows the standard correspondence between second-order
    differential operators and quadratic forms on the cotangent bundle~\cite{Beau2026c,Beau2026a}.
    The co-metric $g^{\mu\nu}$ is read off from the operator $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$, whose spatial part is supplied by
    [H-L] and [H-lift] and whose temporal part is stipulated.
    The metric tensor obtained in this way is not a fundamental geometric object
    defined at the level of the substrate~$\chi$, but an effective structure arising
    from the admissible projection of spectral data onto the continuum limit;
    it carries no ontological status beyond this projection.
    This extraction is canonical once the effective operator $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is fixed: the principal symbol
    determines $g^{\mu\nu}$ exactly, and the freedom lies in the coefficients $A_\tau$ and $A_H$.

    \begin{theorem}[Metric extraction; conditional on {[H-L]} and {[H-lift]}]
      \label{thm:metric}
      Under Hypotheses~[H-L] and [H-lift], the operator $\LPi = -A\partial_x^2$ is the kinetic image of
      $-A_H\DeltaH$ with $A_H = A$ (Remark~\ref{rem:shadow}), and the effective operator $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ on
      $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$ is given by~\eqref{eq:leff}.
      Its principal symbol defines, via~\eqref{eq:op-metric}, a leading-order effective
      co-metric tensor, which in the left-invariant frame $(\partial_\tau, \tilde X, \tilde Y, \tilde Z)$ is
      \begin{equation}
        g^{\mu\nu}(p) = \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau,\; A_H,\; A_H,\; 0)
        \label{eq:metric-tensor}
      \end{equation}
      on $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$.
      This tensor has rank three; no term of lower differential order in $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ changes it, and a
      non-degenerate co-metric requires a new operator (Remark~\ref{rem:zdirection} and open problem Q5b-O2).
      In the homogeneous isotropic regime $(x, y) \approx 0$ (near the identity of $\HeisR$),
      the off-diagonal terms in~\eqref{eq:symbol-expanded} vanish and the symbol simplifies to
      $\sigma_2(L_{\mathrm{eff}}) \approx -A_\tau k_\tau^2 + A_H (k_x^2 + k_y^2)$,
      giving a three-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian symbol at leading order.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{remark}[Status of the lifting hypothesis]
      \label{rem:q9lift}
      Hypothesis~[H-lift] is not discharged.
      Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} proves that, under its premises [K] and [R], the forms Mosco-converge to the kinetic form,
      so that with a supplied representation the limit agrees with the kinetic-sector form (Q9 Corollary~6.1), and it
      states that this does not remove [H-lift] from the present construction.
      Theorem~\ref{thm:metric} is therefore conditional on both [H-L] and [H-lift].
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Status of the $z$-direction]
      \label{rem:zdirection}
      The sub-Riemannian symbol~\eqref{eq:symbol-sublaplacian} has rank two at every point.
      The central direction enters it only through the position-dependent coefficients of $\tilde X$ and $\tilde Y$;
      $\DeltaH$ has no first-order part, and no term of lower differential order changes its principal symbol
      (Q8~\cite{BeauQ8}, Proposition~3.1).
      Under the dilations~\eqref{eq:dilations}, $\DeltaH$ is homogeneous of degree two and $\tilde Z^2$ of degree four;
      a left-invariant operator whose co-metric has a positive constant central coefficient must contain a term
      $\tilde Z^2$, and the ratio of central to horizontal coefficients then depends on the dilation scale, so that
      their equality selects a length scale (Q8, Proposition~5.1 and Remark~5.2).
      No value of $A_z$ is established: for a supplied positive rank-three form invariant under the full
      $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ action, Q8 shows $A_H = A_z$ with the common value free (Q8, Proposition~4.2).
      Open problem Q5b-O2 is open.
      The distinction between the topological dimension $3$ of $\HeisR$ and its Hausdorff dimension $\Dhom = 4$
      comes from the weights of the stratification, not from terms of the symbol.
      The Lorentzian signature result (Section~\ref{sec:lorentz}) applies to the non-degenerate block
      $(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H)$.
      Crucially, the effective four-dimensionality of the geometry arises from the homogeneous
      (Hausdorff and spectral) dimension $\Dhom = 4$ of the Carnot structure, not from a
      non-degenerate Riemannian metric at the principal-symbol level; these two notions of
      dimension must be carefully distinguished.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Nature of the effective four-dimensionality]
      \label{rem:eff4d}
      The effective four-dimensionality obtained in this paper is not that of a
      non-degenerate pseudo-Riemannian manifold at the principal-symbol level.
      It arises from the homogeneous dimension $\Dhom = 4$ of the underlying Carnot
      structure, which governs both volume growth and spectral scaling, as established
      in Section~\ref{sec:heis}.
      The co-metric extracted from the principal symbol is therefore an effective
      object, valid after projection of the discrete admissibility structure onto the
      admissible continuum configurations; it does not claim to be a smooth metric at
      every point of $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$ without further hypotheses.
      A non-degenerate effective co-metric cannot arise from lower-order corrections to
      $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (Q8~\cite{BeauQ8}, Proposition~3.1); it requires a new operator containing a central term
      of homogeneous degree four, together with the length scale that such a term carries, and this is the
      content of open problem Q5b-O2.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Lorentzian Signature under the Hyperbolicity Hypothesis}
  \label{sec:lorentz}

  \subsection{The signature selection argument}

    The following theorem applies the conditional signature analysis of~\cite{Beau2026c} to the part of
    $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$ that the co-metric~\eqref{eq:metric-tensor} does not annihilate.
    In~\cite{Beau2026c}, the capacity bound is the axiom [A-cap], $|\partial_\tau\phi_v| \le c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, and the
    signature selection is conditional on a second-order hyperbolicity hypothesis [H-hyp], which requires a sector
    with a non-degenerate principal symbol and a finite characteristic cone with $\tau$ as the single ordering
    direction; the capacity bound alone does not force hyperbolicity (flux-limited diffusion is a counterexample).
    Since the co-metric~\eqref{eq:metric-tensor} has rank three, [H-hyp] cannot be applied to $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$
    itself; we apply it to its reduction to functions independent of the central variable,
    \begin{equation}
      L_{\mathrm{red}} = -A_\tau \partial_\tau^2 + A_H\bigl(\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2\bigr),
      \label{eq:lred}
    \end{equation}
    whose principal symbol $-A_\tau k_\tau^2 + A_H(k_x^2 + k_y^2)$ is the non-degenerate block.
    We write [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] for [H-hyp] of~\cite{Beau2026c} applied to $L_{\mathrm{red}}$.

    \begin{theorem}[Lorentzian signature; conditional on Theorem~\ref{thm:metric} and on
      {[H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$]}]
      \label{thm:lorentz}
      In homogeneous and quasi-isotropic admissible regimes, and under [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$], the principal
      symbol of $L_{\mathrm{red}}$, which is the non-degenerate block $(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H)$ of the effective
      co-metric~\eqref{eq:metric-tensor}, has signature $(-,+,+)$ with $\tau$ as its time-like direction.
      An extension $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, A_z)$ in the left-invariant frame with $A_z > 0$ would then have
      the Lorentzian signature $(-,+,+,+)$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}[Proof sketch, following the conditional analysis of~{\cite{Beau2026c}}]
Hypothesis [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] posits that $L_{\mathrm{red}}$ is second order in $\tau$, that its principal
symbol is a non-degenerate quadratic form on $(k_\tau, k_x, k_y)$, and that its propagation has a finite
characteristic cone with $\tau$ as the single ordering direction.
A non-degenerate real quadratic form in three variables is, up to an overall sign, either definite or of signature
$(-,+,+)$ with one distinguished direction.
The three classes are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Definite}, $(+,+,+)$ or $(-,-,-)$: the operator is elliptic and has no finite characteristic cone;
  excluded by the finite-cone clause of [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$].
  \item \textbf{Lorentzian with a spatial time-like direction}: the distinguished direction is not $\tau$; excluded by
  the requirement that $\tau$ be the single ordering direction.
  \item \textbf{Degenerate or parabolic}: excluded by the non-degeneracy clause of [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$].
\end{enumerate}
The remaining class is the signature $(-,+,+)$ with $\tau$ time-like, which is that of the stipulated
coefficients $A_\tau, A_H > 0$ in~\eqref{eq:leff}.
The argument does not concern the central direction, where the co-metric~\eqref{eq:metric-tensor} vanishes; no term
of lower differential order changes this (Remark~\ref{rem:zdirection}), and a full-rank extension requires a new
operator (open problem Q5b-O2).
For an extension that is diagonal in the left-invariant frame, a positive central entry $A_z$ leaves $\tau$ as the
only time-like direction; an extension with horizontal--central cross terms need not.
After normalising the maximal admissible propagation speed to $c = 1$, the non-degenerate block is proportional
to $\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1)$ in the homogeneous isotropic regime.
The hyperbolicity hypothesis is an explicit assumption of~\cite{Beau2026c}; it is not derived from the capacity
bound.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Status of the signature]
      \label{rem:emergence}
      The signs of~\eqref{eq:leff} are stipulated, not derived.
      Theorem~\ref{thm:lorentz} records that, under [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$], they are the only ones compatible with
      a finite characteristic cone ordered by $\tau$, on the non-degenerate block.
      The signature so obtained combines three inputs of different status: the supplied Heisenberg carrier, which
      gives $\Dhom = 4$~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}; the projective ordering, supported numerically,
      which gives the direction $\tau$~\cite{Beau2026pto}; and the hyperbolicity hypothesis.
      Under the same hypotheses, and once an extension $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, A_z)$ with $A_z > 0$ is
      supplied (open problem Q5b-O2), the co-metric would be, near the identity and at leading order, proportional to
      the Minkowski form in a maximally symmetric admissible regime; a left-invariant metric on $\HeisR$ is not flat,
      so this is a local statement only.
      This is the conditional content of the claim in~\cite{Cosmochrony} that spacetime is a ``large-$q$ limit of
      the discrete observable space $\mathcal{O}$, with the four-dimensional structure inherited from the homogeneous
      dimension of $G_q$.''
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Ancillary questions and their status}

    Three questions arising naturally from the results of this paper lie outside its scope; their status in the
    companion papers is recorded here.

    \textbf{Q5b-O1} (Proof of [H-lift] via Plancherel decomposition).
    Open.
    Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} proves that, if the kinetic forms Mosco-converge and every finite-energy limit vector admits a
    kinetic recovery sequence on which the modulation energy vanishes, the forms Mosco-converge to the kinetic form;
    with a supplied Schr\"odinger representation the limit then agrees with the kinetic-sector form.
    These premises are not derived from the admissibility data, and Q9 states that its results do not discharge
    [H-lift].
    Theorem~\ref{thm:metric} therefore remains conditional on [H-L] and [H-lift].
    The Plancherel approach outlined in Remark~\ref{rem:harmonic-analysis} remains a possible route.

    \textbf{Q5b-O2} (Extension to a full $4 \times 4$ Lorentzian metric).
    Open.
    Q8~\cite{BeauQ8} proves that the Heisenberg commutator supplies no central coefficient: the sub-Laplacian has no
    first-order part and a rank-two principal symbol, which lower-order terms do not change.
    A left-invariant completion with a positive constant central coefficient must contain a term $\tilde Z^2$ of
    homogeneous degree four, and equality of central and horizontal coefficients selects a length scale.
    For a supplied positive rank-three form invariant under the full $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ action of the spin-one
    module $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$ of Q7~\cite{Beau2026q7}, $A_H = A_z$ with the common value free: the Casimir
    eigenvalue $2$ fixes a normalisation, not a coefficient.

    \textbf{Q5b-O3} (Identification of $A_\tau$ and $A_H$ from admissibility data).
    \label{ssec:resolved-o3}
    Open.
    Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10} proves a conditional isotropy statement: under a uniformity hypothesis on the fingerprint
    observable, a sector assignment, a linear response law and a supplied rank-three target, the ratio $A_H/A_z$
    tends to one; the absolute value follows only from an independently supplied scale, and no value of $A_H$ is
    derived.
    Hypothesis [U] is open~\cite{Beau2026u1}.
    Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11} identifies $A_\tau = 2$ through the Casimir normalisation; by the scale freedom recorded
    under Q5b-O2, that normalisation fixes no coefficient, so no value of $A_\tau$ is established on the inputs
    available here.
    Conditionally on [H-L], [H-lift] and the hyperbolicity hypothesis [H-hyp] of~\cite{Beau2026c} applied to the
    reduced operator~\eqref{eq:lred} (Theorem~\ref{thm:lorentz}), the non-degenerate block of the co-metric has
    signature $(-,+,+)$.
    Establishing [H-L], or replacing it, and constructing a full-rank extension are the open content of Q5.

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  \subsection{What this paper establishes}

    On balls of radius $n \ll \sqrt q$, the BFS shells of $\HeisZ$ converge to the sphere foliation of a sub-Finsler
    Carnot--Carath\'eodory metric on $\HeisR$, whose
    homogeneous dimension is four (Theorem~\ref{thm:carnot}).
    Under the two open hypotheses [H-L] and [H-lift], the effective operator on $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$ has a
    rank-three co-metric $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, 0)$ in the left-invariant frame
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:metric}), and under [H-hyp] applied to its $z$-independent reduction its non-degenerate
    block has signature $(-,+,+)$
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:lorentz}).
    The central slot is not filled by lower-order terms, and no metric coefficient has an established value.

  \subsection{Interpretive outlook}

    \emph{This subsection is interpretation, not a result.}
    The emergent space of the programme is four-dimensional in two distinct senses that the paper keeps apart: as a
    manifold $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \HeisR$, and through the homogeneous dimension of its spatial factor, which
    governs volume growth and heat-kernel decay.
    What is not yet emergent is the metric in the central direction: the non-commutativity of the horizontal
    directions makes the central direction reachable, but assigns it no length.
    For a reader situating the programme, the geometric shape of the effective spacetime is structurally supported,
    while its full metric, its scale and the hypotheses [H-L] and [H-lift] that connect it to the admissibility data
    remain to be constructed.
